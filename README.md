# Pattern of North Atlantic convection preconditions AMOC for non-linear responses to meltwater

The stability of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) under future climate change remains highly uncertain. Current projections generally indicate a relatively muted and quasi-linear AMOC response to freshwater forcing, suggesting a limited role for Greenland ice-sheet melt compared with anthropogenic warming. In this study, we show that differences in patterns of North Atlantic Deep water formation, which is strongly influenced by ice sheet geometry, can alter the AMOC sensitivity to ice melting, \underline{decoupling} AMOC from freshwater forcing to produce abrupt, non-linear responses.

We investigate this behaviour using a new set of coupled atmosphere-ocean climate model simulations (HadCM3) of the early Last Deglaciation (21 to 11 thousand years before present), a period marked by major reorganisations of the Atlantic Ocean circulation and abrupt climate change. The simulations reveal that North Atlantic deep-water formation sites are highly sensitive to ice-sheet geometry, which, through interactions with the atmosphere, effectively controls ocean susceptibility to ice melt. %the ocean response to meltwater release.

In a narrow range of boundary conditions, the climate system follows a hysteretic pathway characterised by abrupt AMOC transitions that reproduce key characteristics of millennial-scale climate variability during the Last Glacial Period, such as Dansgaard-Oeschger cycles. These results demonstrate that the Atlantic overturning circulation sensitivity to freshwater depends not only on the location and amplitude of the forcing, but also on the configuration of North Atlantic deep-water formation. Given the uncertainties around past and future climate conditions, our findings argue for a shift from a forcing-centred to a process-centred approach to past and future Atlantic overturning vulnerability.
